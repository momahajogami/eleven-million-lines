### Mathematics conversation
Worked through:
- Why y = x² does not produce a torus (it's just ℂ)
- Why y² = cubic does — four branch points, two cuts, two sheets glued → Euler characteristic 0 → torus
- The Riemann-Hurwitz formula as a cell count: vertices (branch points, pinched), edges (cuts and free arcs), faces (sheets)
- Betti numbers and cellular homology falling out of the same CW complex structure
- This conversation directly informed the Unit 08 and 07 thinking

### Unit 09 — Quake and the Grammar of Games
Created `07/quake.md` — bridge document situating Quake in Unit 07's mathematical context (BSP as topological decomposition, fast inverse square root as applied geometry, Carmack vs Grothendieck as character contrast).

Created `09/UNIT-09.md` — full unit vision:
- Central argument: the final level of any video game is coding
- Writing/access thesis: writing requires inner skill + interpersonal familiarity + permission + access to technology; games give you the device but not the skill; coding removes the wall
- Gamification critique: physical access to electronics without the mental experience of coding
- Repository ecosystem: Quake source (GPL), Wolf3D, Doom, QuakeC, GoldSrc derivatives, id Tech 3, Abrash's Black Book, Carmack's .plan files, SDL, OpenGL
- Characters: Carmack, Romero, Miyamoto, Roberta Williams, Will Wright

Created `09/other-games/GAMES.md` — history of games as writing technology:
- Pong (1972) — the two-way channel
- Space Invaders (1978) — hardware constraints as drama (accidental difficulty curve)
- Hunt the Wumpus (1973) — first game with topology (dodecahedron cave)
- Colossal Cave Adventure (1976) — first text adventure, computer as storytelling machine
- Zork (1977) — natural language parser, computer as interlocutor
- Pac-Man (1980) — character as algorithm (ghost AI personalities)
- Donkey Kong (1981) — first platformer with narrative arc
- Super Mario Bros (1985) — spatial prose, tutorial without tutorial
- Legend of Zelda (1986) — map as novel, exploration as reading
- Tetris (1984) — pure puzzle, rights battles as story
- Doom (1993) — WAD format, engine/content separation, first great modding platform
- Minecraft (2011) — the sandbox that almost opened the door
