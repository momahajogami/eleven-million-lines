The final level of any video game is coding.

Unit 09 follows id Software from 1991 to 1996: Wolf3D, Doom, Quake. Three games, one decade, one question asked three times with increasing ambition — *how do you decide what not to draw?* The answer evolves from raycasting to BSP trees to the Quake engine, and each step is a lesson in applied topology that Grothendieck would recognize.

John Carmack's .plan files are public mathematical thinking — engineering journals posted to the internet before blogs existed. The fast inverse square root in Quake III is six lines of C that have generated twenty years of commentary. The BSP tree is simplicial homology with a frame budget.

The unit also steps sideways: Tetris as the irreducible game, a playable Python implementation in 200 lines. And the Grassmannian — the space of all possible camera orientations — as the pure mathematics underneath the rendering pipeline.
