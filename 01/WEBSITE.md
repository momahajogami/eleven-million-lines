The place where most of what you use was invented.

Three repositories read alongside a single book: Lions' Commentary on UNIX with Source Code. This is the ground floor. Everything in the course is downstream of what happened here between 1969 and 1975 in a room at Bell Labs.

The unit teaches orientation, not mastery. How do you stand inside a large, old codebase and find your bearings? How do you read an operating system? What does it mean that the same ideas — the file, the process, the pipe — are still the ideas your computer runs on?

## Materials

- **xv6** (`xv6/`) — MIT's teaching OS, a clean reimplementation of Unix v6 in modern C. The primary reading text.
- **Unix v6** (`unix-v6/`) — the original, 1975. About 9,000 lines of C and assembly.
- **Plan 9** (`plan9/`) — Bell Labs, 1992. Unix taken further: everything is a file, including the network.
- **Lions' Commentary** — paired text. John Lions' line-by-line commentary on Unix v6, written for students in 1977, suppressed for twenty years, now free.
- **commentary/xv6/** — course notes on entry points: `main.c`, `initcode.S`, `fork()`, the scheduler
