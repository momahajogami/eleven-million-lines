You are in the right place.

This is a library. It is a very big library. The books go from floor to ceiling and there are eleven of them — eleven big book cases, each one full of something different. Some book cases have music on them. Some have pictures. Some have numbers and shapes. And some of the shelves have code.

Code is a kind of writing. People wrote it, just like people write books and songs. You can read it too — not all of it, not yet, but some of it. And even the parts you can't read yet, you can look at. You can hold the book. You can know it's there.

That is what this course is. It is a visit to a library where very important things were written. We are going to look at them together.

Some of the things in this library are very old. The first ones were written before your parents were born. Before your grandparents were born. Some of the people who wrote them are not alive anymore. But the writing is still here. That is what writing does.

Here are some of the people who wrote things in this library:

Ken Thompson wrote an operating system. An operating system is the thing that makes a computer go. He wrote it in a room in New Jersey a long time ago, and almost every phone and computer in the world still uses something from what he wrote.

Ada Lovelace wrote the very first program. She was a mathematician and she lived in the 1800s. She figured out that a machine could follow instructions — not just do arithmetic, but follow a sequence. She wrote it down. We still have the paper.

Richard Stallman was very upset that a printer was broken and nobody would share the code to fix it. So he decided that code should always be shared. He wrote a whole philosophy about it, and then he wrote a whole operating system to prove it.

Linus Torvalds wrote a note to the internet that said, essentially: I'm making an operating system, it's probably not very good, what do you think? Millions of people helped him finish it. It runs almost everything now.

These people are characters. They have personalities. They argued and created and sometimes made each other angry and sometimes made each other laugh. That is all still in the writing.

You don't have to understand everything to be here.

You just have to be curious.

When you look at a page of code and think I wonder what that does — that is the right feeling. That is the feeling that belongs here. Hold onto it.

One day you will come back to something you saw today and it will make sense. That is how libraries work.
