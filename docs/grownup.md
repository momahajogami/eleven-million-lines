**Eleven Million Lines You Should Know** is a course for young children and the families who care for them — reading landmark codebases the way you read a very good book: slowly, attentively, together.

Eleven codebases. Early Unix. C compilers. GNU. Blender. LaTeX. Haskell. Assembly. The first programs Ada Lovelace ever wrote. The mathematics of Grothendieck and Lawvere made executable.

The course is paired with primary sources: Lions' Commentary, the GNU Manifesto, Turing's 1936 paper, Lovelace's 1843 notes. Code is not separate from culture. It never was.

It can also be taught at university — and will be. But it starts here, with the youngest readers, because that is the right place to start.

- First venue: Ohio
- All materials open source
- Designed for young children, families, and anyone who wants to go back to the source
