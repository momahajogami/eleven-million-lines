Classical Coding: the compiler, the editor, the version control system, and the man who built two of the most important things in software history.

Unit 02 pairs the tools with the tradition. You cannot read the Unix source without an editor; you cannot build it without a compiler; you cannot collaborate on it without version control. These tools are also codebases — some of the most carefully written in existence.

The unit closes with Linus Torvalds: the author of Linux and of git, which is extraordinary. Two foundational systems, one person, twenty years apart. The LINUS.md character study asks what kind of mind produces both.

## Materials

- **tcc** (`tcc/`) — Fabrice Bellard's Tiny C Compiler. ~15,000 lines. Compiles itself. The cleanest introduction to how a compiler works.
- **gcc** (`gcc/`) — the GNU Compiler Collection. Sparse checkout of the core. The production compiler that built the internet.
- **vim** (`vim/`) — Bill Joy's editor, maintained by Bram Moolenaar for thirty years. Read `src/normal.c` for the command parser.
- **git** (`git/`) — Linus Torvalds, 2005. Written in ten days after BitKeeper revoked its license. Sparse checkout of the core.
- **LINUS.md** — character study: Linux and git, one person, two founding contributions, one uncompromising voice
- **ZETTELKASTEN.md** — Zettelkasten and Unix as parallel systems: the acoustic and electronic versions of the same idea
