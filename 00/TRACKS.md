# The Two Tracks

*The classical experience is worth having. We don't pretend all tools are equal. But we don't lock the door either.*

---

## Track A — Classical

You will work in a Unix environment. You will navigate the terminal. You will edit in vim or ed. You will compile the code yourself, run the binaries yourself, read the errors yourself.

This is not hazing. It is the curriculum.

The tools of Track A are part of what you are studying. Unix grew up in an environment where these tools were the environment — where the programmers who wrote the code also wrote the editors and compilers they used to write it. The code and the tools are made of the same stuff. Reading them together, in the tradition they emerged from, is different from reading them outside it.

A student who reads Carmack's BSP tree renderer in vim, in a terminal, with a compiled build running next to it — who moves between the source and the running program with keyboard commands, who has spent weeks in the environment that produced the code — understands something that cannot be fully transmitted through a different interface. Not because the interface matters abstractly, but because embodied practice changes comprehension.

**What Track A requires:**

- Comfort navigating the filesystem from the command line
- Basic vim (or ed) competence — enough to open, edit, and save a file
- Ability to compile a program with `make` or `cc`
- Willingness to be uncomfortable until you are comfortable

The first two weeks of the course serve as initiation for students who need them. You will learn what you need.

**What Track A gives you:**

Inhabitation of the tradition. The feeling of being inside the environment that produced the code, rather than looking at it from outside. This is hard to describe to someone who has not experienced it, and obvious to someone who has.

---

## Track B — Accompanied

You bring your environment. We meet you there.

Track B students use whatever editor and operating system they arrived with. They receive more editorial guidance through difficult passages, more scaffolding around the technical setup, more explicit signposting of where to look and why.

Track B is not a lesser version of the course. The ideas are the same. The codebases are the same. The primary sources are the same. The intellectual demands are the same.

What Track B is honest about: something is different. Reading Unix source in VS Code on Windows is not the same experience as reading it in vim on Linux. Both experiences can lead to genuine understanding — but they are not the same experience. Track B does not pretend otherwise.

**What Track B requires:**

- Any computer with internet access
- Willingness to engage with code directly, not just read about it
- Acceptance that some of the traditional experience will be mediated differently

**What Track B gives you:**

The ideas. The tradition — not inhabited, but known. The understanding that a tradition exists, what it looks like, and that you are now in relationship to it. That relationship is real even if it is not the full immersion of Track A.

---

## What both tracks share

**The same standard.** The code you are expected to read, the primary sources you are expected to engage with, the intellectual demands on your attention — these do not vary by track.

**The same orientation.** The course has a point of view: the classical tradition is worth knowing. This is not an insult to students who come without it, and it is not a license for condescension from students who have it. It is an acknowledgment that the tools and practices we study did not appear from nowhere — they grew from specific people solving specific problems in a specific culture — and understanding them fully requires understanding that context.

**The same invitation.** Everyone who wants to read this code is welcome. No one is excluded from the ideas.

The door is open. Track A is the door you walk through to stand in the original room. Track B is the door you walk through to see the original room through good glass. Both are worth walking through.

---

*If you are unsure which track you are on: start with Track A and ask for help when you need it. The discomfort of Track A is temporary. The understanding it builds is not.*
